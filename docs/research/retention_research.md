# Retention Research: Returning Players in PBBGs

> Source: r/PBBG discussion — "Returning Players - How to get them?" (Aug 2026, 42 comments)

---

## Core Insight

**The real challenge isn't bringing players back — it's giving them a reason to never leave.** Once someone quits, they're usually gone for good. Winning back a former player is far more difficult than acquiring a new one.

---

## Key Takeaways (by theme)

### 1. "Numbers Go Up" Isn't Enough
- A player who logs in, clicks 3 buttons to collect rewards and set idle tasks, then leaves is **not meaningfully retained**
- Mechanical engagement (collect → idle → close) eventually burns out
- Track **session length + meaningful actions**, not just logins
- "A player chatting for 10 minutes without clicking a game button is infinitely more engaged than a silent idle-scrapper"

### 2. Community > Mechanics
- **The #1 retention driver is social connection** — groups, rivals, people you committed to showing up for
- "People come back for two reasons: there's always something new, and the game never betrays its core"
- "It's not about building a game we'd want to play ourselves. It's building a place where players feel at home"
- D&D analogy: "The rules aren't why it lasted. The table is."
- Watch whether players are **talking to each other, helping newcomers, showing up in community even outside the game**
- That's when they stop being customers and start being **members**

### 3. Interdependent Ecosystems Hook Different Player Types
- Games like EVE, Foxhole: **every action matters, things used have to be made by others**
- PVE players love building/selling/supplying to PVP players
- Creates a loop: check markets → see what allies need → move product → profit
- "I will keep the game open on a second monitor because I like seeing what's going on"
- **Relevance to VS**: Freighter routes, trade stations, alliance supply chains already support this

### 4. Dynamic Goals Beat Static Progression
- "If the whole game can be automated or scheduled for 24 hours, the player's connection becomes completely passive"
- Break the "log in, dump, leave" cycle with **dynamic goals**
- Seasonal events with free cosmetics drive returns
- "Missions and completeness is what drives me personally"
- Having something to **chase** (medals, milestones, leaderboard spot to defend) — the timer is the excuse to open the tab, the hook is the reason to stay

### 5. Don't Be Too Demanding
- "If the game is too demanding but really enjoyable, it's hard to leave but physically and mentally needed"
- One player burned out on PBBGs by playing too many at once — timer management across games
- Finding the right balance: engaging but **not stressing if you miss a day or two**
- Avoid "play everyday or fall behind" — this is why MMOs lost to match-based games (MOBAs, shooters)

### 6. Returning After a Break
- "Returning to a familiar game with unfamiliar players is oddly unsettling"
- Players who quit often find the style of game is no longer what they're looking for
- **Password recovery / progress preservation is critical** — don't force restarts
- If friends are gone, the pull is gone

### 7. Communication / Re-engagement
- Email marketing: divisive — some hate any email, others want multiple per day
- In-game notifications feel less forced than emails
- Best email trigger: **new server/world starts** or major updates
- Referral programs with personal invite links worked for one dev
- Keep email optional for registration — mandatory = friction

### 8. Measurement
- Don't trust login counts — they're "fake retention"
- Better metrics:
  - **Session length** (20-30 min of chatting/trading/goal-pursuing >> 45 sec daily check-in)
  - **Chat activity** (Discord, in-game)
  - **"Who's chasing something or tied to someone"** — predicts retention better than session counts
  - Community participation outside the game

---

## Pareto's Principle (from FvDijk / Aether Kingdoms)

- 80% of players don't come back
- 80% of the remaining 20% don't join Discord
- 80% of that 20% don't actively participate
- The final few: contribute to wiki, build community tools, help keep others engaged

### Our Reddit Advantage
Unlike standalone PBBGs, we don't need email marketing or Discord — **the subreddit IS the community hub**. User flair, achievement posts, and leaderboard comments all happen where Reddit users already live. The game never asks them to go somewhere else.
